About me

My favorite part of college essay season is getting to really know my students.

Somewhere between the first brainstorm and the final draft, a seventeen-year-old figures out what they actually want to say about who they are. That's when the essay comes alive, and it's when I see them start to believe in the person they're about to become.

I've spent 20 years teaching English, including 10 years of AP Literature, where my students' pass rates consistently beat state and global averages. I also teach a course on college applications and scholarship essays. My goal is to help every student walk into the next stage of life confident in their own voice.

Questions families ask

Don't see yours? Email or text me.

Do you write or edit the essay for my student?

No. Students write every word. I ask questions that get to what matters and give honest feedback on structure, voice and clarity. Admissions readers can tell when an adult wrote an essay.

Is it too late to get help?

Not at all. Many students make their biggest improvements in the final weeks. Even one session can turn a decent essay into a strong one.

How many sessions will we need?

It depends on how far along your student is. A polished draft may need one session. A student starting from scratch usually needs two or three.

Are sessions online or in person?

All sessions are on Zoom, so students can join from anywhere, even while traveling over winter break. We work in a shared Google Doc so we can look at the draft together and you can see my comments in real time.

How do I pay?

By card when you book. Payment is handled securely by Stripe, and you'll get a receipt by email.

How fast is an essay review?

The calendar only offers call times after your written notes will be ready, so pick any open time. Near deadlines, book early: spots fill quickly the week before January 1.

Can you help with scholarship essays too?

Yes. I teach a course on college applications and scholarship essays, and sessions can cover those too.
